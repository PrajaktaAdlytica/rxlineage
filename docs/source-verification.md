Verified 16 October 2026 using the configured Perplexity `perplexity_research` high preset, restricted in the research request to official AHRQ, Surescripts, DrFirst and FDA material. Direct `curl -L` retrieval independently checked the six destinations below. This is source verification for a research-stage concept, not clinical validation or a regulatory opinion.

## Availability and changes

- The four URLs already present in the IA were retrieved by Perplexity; none was reported as a dead page. No replacement URL was identified.
- Direct final responses were HTTP 200 for Surescripts, DrFirst, the FDA guidance page and the FDA PDF, with the same final URLs listed above.
- Direct automated requests to both AHRQ pages returned HTTP 403. Perplexity could retrieve their official contents. Record this as an automated-access limitation, not a confirmed broken URL; the two AHRQ sources still need an ordinary-browser check before a public launch.
- Perplexity did not expose redirect chains. The direct checks above report final destinations only and must not be described as a complete redirect audit.
- The IA’s broad “January 2026 guidance” remains correct. If an exact FDA issue date is displayed, use **29 January 2026**, distinguishing it from the superseded **6 January 2026** version and from later web retrieval/indexing dates.

## Website claim boundaries

The sourced description is: **“Proposed source-attributed discrepancy queue for pharmacist adjudication; synthetic demonstration only.”** The website may show dated source comparisons, uncertainty, abstention, draft questions, named fictional reviewer roles and illustrative audit states. These are proposed workflows and synthetic examples, not evidence of product performance.

Keep all five offers labeled as proposed. Keep “for pharmacist adjudication” visible on issue prioritization. Do not show independent medication-change recommendations, automatic clinical actions or sent patient/prescriber messages. An unsent draft remains explicitly unsent. No clinical efficacy, medication-error savings, completeness, compliance, reimbursement, live integration, partner, customer or regulatory-classification claim is supported by these sources.

The hypothetical test price remains **$3,000 per hospital site per month plus $8,000 evaluation setup**; these sources do not validate buyer acceptance or economics. Readiness remains conditional on 8 pharmacist/director interviews, blinded-case review approval, 1 pharmacy champion, 1 funded silent-mode scope and signed data-access/security and human-oversight approvals. Primary evaluation: pharmacist-adjudicated precision at a prespecified queue size; guardrails: missed critical discrepancies, false-alert burden and review time, compared with the agreed baseline. Stop or reposition if critical cases are missed or incremental precision over existing history/EHR alerts does not justify pharmacist effort. These gates and metrics come from the project brief, not from the external sources.
